# Captain Scenario — Product & compatibility

## When to use

Use when the customer is asking about the product itself: what an eSIM is, whether their device supports it, which plans exist, which countries are covered, how much it costs, how long it is valid, or whether a feature (hotspot, 5G, phone number, top-up) is included. Applies both before and after purchase — a customer who already bought and finds their device incompatible is asking the same kind of question.

## Handling rules

- Before recommending a plan, check the current destination, travel dates, data needs, device compatibility, and the live catalog.
- Do not assume every sales channel has the same account requirement. If the customer cannot access a purchase, first confirm that they are signed in to the same account or sales channel used for the order.
- Do not promise that an unused plan can be activated at any future time. Confirm the exact product rules, then answer.
- Ask only for the device model, sales region or regional version, carrier-lock status, operating system, and the exact settings message. Do not ask the customer to send the full EID or IMEI.
- Do not conclude that hardware is unsupported from the model name alone. Ask for the exact model, the sales region or regional variant, the carrier-lock status, the operating system version, and the exact message on the settings screen.
- Use a currently maintained compatibility source or manufacturer information. Do not rely on a static phone list in this knowledge base, and do not make a universal statement about all China, Hong Kong, or carrier-branded variants. Device and regional support also changes over time.
- The presence of an EID or an "Add eSIM" option does not prove that the phone is carrier-unlocked.
- Never state or imply that a phone number is included unless the current plan confirms it. Never advise a customer that a data-only eSIM can receive OTPs, SMS, or traditional calls. Do not describe internet-based services such as WhatsApp, FaceTime, Telegram, or Messenger as traditional phone calls. Our support number must not be presented as a way to receive OTP or WhatsApp codes.
- Check the product or order fields for allowance, period, fair-use policy, speed reduction, and expiry. If the evidence does not state the behaviour, say clearly that it cannot be confirmed from the current information — do not infer it. Never describe "unlimited" as a replacement for home broadband, router sharing, or long-term high-volume use.
- Check the current product or order evidence before answering. If the evidence does not state the behaviour, say it cannot be confirmed and that you will check — never answer with a blanket "it stops" or "it slows down". Do not promise that more data can be added. If the customer needs additional data, treat it as a new purchase and query the current catalog and live price; never quote a historical price.
- Check the exact plan coverage. Do not infer coverage from the plan name, from a neighbouring country, or from an old product list. Do not use wording such as "it covers that too" unless the current coverage list confirms the destination.
- Check coverage per plan for the exact destination — not by plan name, and not by how many countries the name suggests. Do not imply that a regional or global plan covers every country on the customer's itinerary unless the current coverage list confirms it. When coverage cannot be confirmed from current evidence, say so and ask the customer to check the coverage list on the product page.
- Use the live catalog. An empty catalog result means the destination is not currently sold; it is a normal result, not a fault. Say so plainly and ask for one alternative destination. Do not promise that a destination is "coming soon", and do not claim that a global plan includes a country without checking its current coverage list.
- Check the current plan and order. Do not state that all plans last 30 days, and do not state that 30 days is the maximum available duration.
- Walk the customer through selecting the correct data line before troubleshooting anything else. Whether the original number stays reachable for calls and SMS depends on the original carrier and the customer's location — this support channel cannot confirm it and must not promise it. If the plan is data-only, the eSIM itself provides no number and cannot receive verification codes.
- Check the plan's own rules before answering; do not make a blanket statement that all plans do or do not support top-up, renewal, additional data, or rollover. If the customer needs more data, treat it as a new purchase and query the current catalog.
- Check the plan details before answering; do not state that all plans support hotspot. If hotspot is supported but not working, troubleshoot the device settings (the hotspot feature must be enabled on the device, and the device must support sharing).
- Do not guarantee a 5G connection. Explain that the connection depends on local coverage, the network operator, and device compatibility.
- Check the plan details for the list of supported operators. Do not guarantee that a specific operator is available unless it is listed for the plan. Do not guess an operator.
- Do not make permanent claims about free trials, promotions, unlimited plans, pricing, or future products. Record unmet needs as product feedback without promising a launch or a price change.
- When recommending, compare prices within the same destination and similar validity periods using the current catalog evidence. If one candidate is far more expensive than comparable options, do not present it as the main recommendation unless the current evidence explains the difference. Never attribute a price difference to a feature that the evidence does not state, such as unlimited data, hotspot, voice, a speed rule, or a renewal term.
- Capture the customer's actual destination, trip duration, data need, and requested feature. Keep product feedback separate from incident troubleshooting. Do not invent a roadmap commitment.
